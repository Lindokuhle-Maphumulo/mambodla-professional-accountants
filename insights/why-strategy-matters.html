Why strategy matters more than ever

Strategy gives a business something more valuable than a plan: a clear basis for choosing where to focus, what to protect and which opportunities are worth pursuing.

03 Sep 2026 4 min read Mambodla Professional Accountants

Businesses make decisions every day: what to sell, where to invest, which customers to pursue, what to stop doing and how to respond when conditions change.

Without a clear strategy, those decisions can become disconnected. A business may stay busy while moving in several directions at once. Strategy creates a shared point of reference. It helps management decide what matters most and directs limited time, money and attention toward the priorities that support long-term value.

01

Give the business a clear direction

A strategy should answer a few important questions clearly: what kind of business are we building, who do we want to serve, what value do we offer and what must we do particularly well to compete?

When these answers are understood, everyday decisions become easier to evaluate. Opportunities can be tested against a clear direction rather than judged only by how attractive they appear in the moment.

Direction also helps teams understand why some priorities receive more attention than others. People are more likely to make aligned decisions when they know what the organisation is trying to achieve.

02

Turn limited resources into deliberate priorities

Every business operates with constraints. Capital, management time, skills and operational capacity are never unlimited.

Strategy forces choices. If everything is a priority, nothing really is. Management should be able to identify the few areas that deserve disproportionate focus because they create the greatest value or protect the business from the greatest risk.

This discipline can prevent resources from being spread across too many initiatives, each receiving too little attention to deliver meaningful results.

Key takeaway

Strategy is as much about deciding what not to do as it is about deciding what to pursue.

03

Connect strategy to financial reality

A strategy becomes useful only when the numbers support it.

Growth plans may require additional staff, equipment, working capital or marketing spend. New products may take time to become profitable. Entering a different market may create costs before it creates revenue.

Financial forecasts help management test whether strategic choices are affordable, what assumptions must hold true and how much risk the business is taking. This is where accounting information moves beyond reporting history and becomes part of forward-looking decision-making.

04

Stay adaptable without losing direction

Good strategy is not rigid. Markets change, customer expectations evolve and new risks or opportunities emerge.

The objective is not to follow an old plan simply because it was written down. It is to keep the underlying direction clear while adjusting actions when evidence changes.

Regular strategic reviews can help leaders ask whether assumptions still hold, whether priorities remain relevant and whether the business is learning from actual results. Adaptability becomes stronger when it is guided by purpose rather than reaction.

05

Make execution part of the strategy

A strategy that lives only in a document does not change a business.

The priorities need owners, measures, timelines and regular review. Management should know which indicators show whether the strategy is working and where progress is falling behind.

Useful questions include:

  • What are our three most important priorities right now?
  • Which financial measures show whether those priorities are creating value?
  • Who is accountable for each major initiative?
  • What assumptions are we relying on?
  • What would cause us to change course?
  • What have we deliberately chosen not to pursue?

Strategy becomes powerful when choices, resources and accountability all point in the same direction.

Mambodla Perspective

Strategy is not a document. It is the discipline of choosing what matters most.

Strong businesses connect ambition to evidence. They understand their numbers, make deliberate choices and review those choices as conditions change. Strategy gives those decisions a common direction and turns activity into purposeful progress.
