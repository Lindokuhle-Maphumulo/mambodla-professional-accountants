Building a resilient business in uncertain times

Resilience is not about avoiding uncertainty. It is about building the financial visibility, systems and decision-making discipline needed to respond to change with confidence.

08 May 2025 6 min read Mambodla Professional Accountants

Every business faces uncertainty. Economic conditions change, customer needs evolve, costs rise and new challenges often appear without warning. What separates a resilient business from a vulnerable one is not the absence of difficulty, but the ability to understand what is happening, make informed decisions and respond without losing sight of long-term objectives.

Resilience therefore begins long before a crisis arrives. It is built through stronger financial information, disciplined planning, reliable systems and a clear understanding of what truly drives the business.

01

Understand what really drives the business

A resilient business understands where its value comes from. That means knowing which products, services and customers contribute most meaningfully to revenue and profitability, where costs are increasing and which parts of the business carry the greatest risk.

When management understands these drivers, difficult decisions become clearer. Resources can be directed toward the areas that matter most rather than being spread across activities that provide limited value.

02

Build stronger financial visibility

Strong decisions depend on reliable information. Business owners should be able to understand their cash position, profitability, upcoming obligations and financial performance without waiting until year-end.

Regular management information, cash-flow monitoring and meaningful financial reporting allow potential problems to be identified earlier. The earlier a challenge becomes visible, the more options the business usually has to respond to it.

Key takeaway

Resilience comes from visibility, preparation and disciplined decision-making.

03

Reduce avoidable risk

Some business risks cannot be controlled, but many can be managed. Weak record keeping, missed compliance obligations, poor payroll processes, unclear responsibilities and inconsistent financial controls can place unnecessary pressure on an organisation.

Reliable processes create stability. They reduce dependence on last-minute decisions and allow management to focus more energy on customers, operations and growth.

04

Plan for more than one scenario

A plan becomes stronger when it recognises that the future may not unfold exactly as expected. Businesses can benefit from considering different possibilities: what happens if revenue grows, remains stable or declines? What if a major cost increases? What if a key customer pays later than expected?

Scenario planning does not require predicting the future perfectly. Its purpose is to help management think ahead, understand possible consequences and identify actions before pressure forces a rushed response.

05

Protect the quality of decision-making

Uncertainty often creates pressure to move quickly. Speed can be valuable, but urgency should not replace good judgement.

Resilient organisations develop the habit of asking the right questions before making significant decisions:

  • What information do we have?
  • What information are we missing?
  • What are the financial consequences?
  • What risks are we accepting?
  • Does this decision support our long-term priorities?

This discipline becomes especially important when conditions are changing quickly.

Mambodla Perspective

Resilience is built before it is tested.

Businesses become stronger when financial information is understood, responsibilities are clear and decisions are made with purpose. The objective is not to eliminate uncertainty. It is to build an organisation capable of navigating it.
